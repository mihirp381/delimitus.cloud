The gap

It works on your laptop. Now what?

Between a tool that works and a link the night shift can open sits a list nobody on the floor signed up for.

  1. A cloud account

    Someone sets it up, pays for it and keeps it apart from everything else.

  2. Company login

    Only staff get in, and people who leave lose access with their account.

  3. Secrets

    The carrier’s API key can’t live in the code or in a shared sheet.

  4. A database

    Backed up, and not shared with every other tool in the building.

  5. An IT review

    Security wants to know what it reaches and who can see it.

  6. Someone to keep it running

    Updates, logs, and the call when it stops at 2 a.m.

So the tool stays on a laptop, or goes back to a spreadsheet. Delimitus is that list, done.

Security

Locked down by default.

Company login on every app

Every request needs a company login. There are no public links.

Walled off

An app can’t reach other apps. It reaches the internet only where IT allowed it.

Secrets stay secret

Stored for the app and never shown again, to people or to coding agents.

Checked before it ships

Every build is checked for leaked keys before it goes live.

Limits

What it doesn’t do.

Some things are left out on purpose.

  • No public appsEvery request needs a company login.
  • No custom domainsApps live at an address on our domain.
  • No inbound webhooksOutside services can’t call into your app.
  • Company data is read-onlyApps read your company databases and never write to them.

Questions

What does it cost?

Pricing is agreed with each pilot. The cost section shows what doing it yourself costs.

Which tools can I build with?

Claude Code, Codex, Cursor, Lovable and Replit. Python and Node apps, from a folder or a GitHub repository.

Where does it run?

On Google Cloud in the US, with each company in a cloud account of its own.

Who can open my app?

Only people signed in with your company login, and only the ones you shared it with: a person, a group, or the whole company.

Can an app be public?

No. Every request needs a company login.

What happens to my code and data?

Your app runs in your company’s own cloud account. It reads company databases read-only and reaches the internet only where IT allowed it. Secrets are never shown again, to people or to coding agents.
